One bet becomes rows in three table types. 03

The mistake that costs you the meeting

Grain — what one row means — is the first modelling decision and the one that breaks everything else if you get it wrong.

Sport belongs to a leg: a four-leg multi across AFL, NRL and two races has no single sport. Stake belongs to the slip: the customer put down one amount. Those are two different grains, so this platform has two fact tables, and stake never appears as an additive measure on the leg one.

Put it there and sum it, and turnover is multiplied by the average number of legs. The number still looks plausible — which is exactly why it survives review, and why it needs a test rather than an eyeball.

The check that catches it

Sum the allocated stake across every one of the 9,359 leg rows. Sum the exact stake across every one of the 6,191 slip rows. They must match to the cent — across any grouping, at any grain.

And the one that catches a missing fixture

Bets were placed on three fixtures whose reference data had not reached the warehouse yet. 142 legs loaded anyway, pointing at the -1 unknown member, so turnover was never understated. When batch 2 brought the fixtures in, the mart rebuild attached them to the real events — no backfill, no correction. 8 remain, and those are genuine orphans: selection ids that appear on bet legs but exist in no pricing feed at all.

Dropping those rows instead would have made the warehouse quietly disagree with the betting engine, which is the worse failure by a distance.

04

Why both models, not one

Data Vault and dimensional modelling are not competitors. They optimise for different things, and neither does the other's job.

Raw vault — built for loading

  • Insert-only, so it is auditable by construction. “What did we know about this customer's self-exclusion status on the 14th” is a query.
  • No business rules. If the CRM said VIC, the satellite says VIC.
  • Loads in parallel, in any order — no hub reads a link.
  • Replayable: the same batch twice inserts nothing.
  • Unqueryable by humans. Reassembling one bet is a hub, a link and two satellites with a latest-version window on each.

Star schema — built for asking

  • One join from fact to dimension. Turnover by sport by channel is a single query an analyst gets right first time.
  • All the business rules live here: age bands, channel groupings, hold %.
  • Type 2 where it earns it. A customer who moves NSW → VIC has two rows, so June's bets stay NSW turnover and the jurisdictional return is right.
  • Rebuilt every run, which is what makes changing a definition cheap.
  • No history it did not derive. Everything traces back to the vault.
The join between them is smaller than it looks

A satellite stores only load_date — when a version started — because writing an end date would mean updating the previous row and breaking the insert-only guarantee. So end dates are derived at read time with one lead() window. An insert-only satellite plus a lead() window is a type 2 dimension. That one function is the whole bridge between the two modelling styles.

dim_customer · one customer, two versions, after a self-exclusion in batch 2

Version 1 starts at 1900, not at the vault's load date. The vault records when we learned something, not when it became true, so without back-dating, every bet placed before the first CRM extract would fail its as-at join. That back-dating is a business rule — which is why it lives in the mart and not in the vault.

What the marts are for

Turnover is money staked. Gross win is turnover minus payouts. Hold % is the margin the book actually realised — and it is the number the trading desk lives on.

Two sports show a negative hold — the book lost money on them over four weeks. That is not a bug: hold is volatile until a very large number of bets have settled, which is why traders watch it against the margin that was priced in rather than against zero. Every market is priced with an overround: the implied probabilities (1 ÷ decimal odds) sum to more than 100%, and the excess is the margin. On a multi the odds multiply, so the margin compounds — three legs at 6% each is closer to 19% than 6%. That single fact explains most of the commercial behaviour of a modern bookmaker, and it falls straight out of the data model.

06

Running it twice changes nothing

The least glamorous property in the whole build, and the one that decides whether you get woken up.

Bronze replaces a batch instead of appending it. Vault hubs and links anti-join on the hash key. Satellites compare a hash_diff and insert only on a real change. Marts are a full rebuild.

So re-running yesterday's job is a no-op rather than an incident, and every task in the Databricks Workflow can carry automatic retries without anyone having to reason about what a partial failure did. That is not a habit — it is designed in, at every layer.

07

The vocabulary

Most of it turns up as column names. A few of these change how you model.

Decimal odds
$10 at 2.50 returns $25 including stake. Implied probability is 1 ÷ odds.
Overround
Implied probabilities across a market sum above 100%. The excess is the margin priced in.
Turnover / handle
Total staked. A customer betting $10 twenty times is $200 of turnover.
Gross win
Turnover minus payouts. Revenue before promotional cost.
GGR
Gross win net of bonuses. What regulators, the ASX and wagering taxes are based on.
Hold %
Gross win ÷ turnover. The realised margin. Single digits and volatile.
Multi / SGM
Several legs, all must win, odds multiply. An SGM's legs are all in one event, so they are correlated.
Bet slip vs leg
One slip, one or many legs. This distinction is the two-fact grain split.
Void / cashed out
Why payout is not simply zero or stake × odds, and why settlement has four statuses.
Self-exclusion
BetStop. Must apply immediately everywhere, while history still shows what came before — an argument for an insert-only vault.
Point of consumption tax
Each state taxes revenue from its own residents, so jurisdiction is a tax calculation, not a report.
Unknown member
The -1 dimension row that lets a fact load when its dimension has not arrived.

To run it
cd betting_lakehouse
make setup     # venv + PySpark 4 + Delta Lake
make run       # two batches, end to end, ~4 minutes
make test      # 48 tests

No Databricks account needed. Real Delta tables, a real metastore, and a real MERGE — on a laptop.

All data synthetic, generated from a fixed seed — which is what makes the idempotency tests mean something. Odds derive from simulated probabilities with a 6.5% overround, so the realised hold converges on a plausible book margin instead of being invented.
